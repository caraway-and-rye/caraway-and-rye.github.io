# King Bakery Peanut Butter Cookies

## Ingredients

**The full batch is the source of truth.** The small batch is exactly ⅛ of it.

| Ingredient   | Full   | Small |
| ------------ | ------ | ----- |
| Brown Sugar  | 1814g  | 227g  |
| Sugar        | 1814g  | 227g  |
| Shortening   | 1814g  | 227g  |
| Peanut Butter| 1814g  | 227g  |
| Eggs         | 946mL (16 large) | 118mL (2 large) |
| Cake Flour   | 2268g  | 283g  |
| Baking Soda  | 70.9g  | 8.9g  |

## How to Make

Cream the brown sugar, sugar, shortening and peanut butter together — **but not too much, or the cookies will spread flat and thin.** About 2 minutes at just above low speed.

Add the eggs and let them mix in.

Add the cake flour on slow speed, then the baking soda.

Bake at **190 °C** (original: 375 °F). Roughly 10–12 minutes. **Don't let the edges get properly brown.**

## Makes

- Full batch → **28 dozen**
- Small batch → **3½ dozen**

## Notes

- **One figure differs from the card**: the small batch's baking soda was written as 2 tsp (≈9.6g). Exactly ⅛ of the full batch is 8.9g, which is what's used above. Everything else in the small batch scaled exactly.
- The four base ingredients are equal weights — 4 lb each at full size, ½ lb each small. Easy to remember, easy to scale to anything.
- **Cake flour, not all-purpose.** Low protein is part of why these stay tender despite the long list of stiff ingredients. Substituting will change the texture.
- Soda only, no baking powder — it's reacting with the brown sugar's acidity, and it's what gives the cracked top.
- The undermixing instruction is the whole recipe. Overcreaming aerates the fat, and aerated fat collapses in the oven into a thin, greasy cookie.
