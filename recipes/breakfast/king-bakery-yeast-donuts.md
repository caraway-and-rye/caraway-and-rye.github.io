## Method

Mix for longer than you think you need to. The gluten really needs to develop — the dough should end up almost *silky*.

## Notes

- **Two errors in the salt row on the original card.** Both are corrected in the table above:

  | Column | Card as written | Should be | Used here |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 6 qt | `7-8` (7 lb 8 oz) | 7½ oz | 213g |
  | 1½ qt | `2-0` (2 lb) | 1⅞ oz | 53.2g |

  `7-8` is almost certainly "7.5" written with a hyphen and then read as pounds-and-ounces. **7 lb 8 oz of salt in a 6 qt batch would be sixteen times the correct dose and completely inedible.** The 1½ qt figure is the same kind of slip. The 4 qt, 2 qt and 1 qt columns all scale exactly from 10 oz at 8 qt, which lands at 1.74% of flour — a normal bakery dose, confirming that 10 oz is the right anchor. (The ¾ qt and ½ qt columns were rounded up on the card to 1 oz and ¾ oz; the table above uses the exact 0.9375 oz and 0.625 oz instead, in line with how roundings are handled elsewhere in the collection.)

- **The 1½ qt column has two further discrepancies**, also corrected above:

  | Ingredient | Card as written | Exact from 8 qt | Used here |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Yeast | 6 oz → 170g | 3 oz | 85g |
  | Shortening | 1 lb → 454g | 1 lb 2 oz | 510g |

  The yeast one matters — 6 oz is double the correct amount and would give you a fast, boozy, over-proofed dough. Milk, eggs, both flours, sugar and baking powder all scale exactly in that column, so the errors are isolated rather than systemic. **If the 1½ qt column was ever hand-tuned deliberately** — it is flagged as the Hobart's max capacity, so it may have been the house batch — say so and I'll restore the card's numbers.

- **The card never says which yeast.** At 2.8% of flour this reads as fresh yeast. If instant, the dose is roughly triple normal.
- Total flour is 36 lb at 8 qt (28 lb bread + 8 lb pastry). Baking powder alongside yeast is deliberate — it gives the lift that makes a raised donut light rather than bready.
- Batch size in quarts means quarts of liquid at 2 lb per quart: 16 lb milk = 8 qt.
- Card note: each egg averages 2 oz and cost 6.7 cents.
