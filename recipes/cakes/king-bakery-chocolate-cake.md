### Frosting variant — 50/50 butter & Sweetex

For a more dairy-forward frosting. As written the fat runs 43.5 / 56.5 butter to Sweetex; stage 2 is already even, so only stage 1 changes:

| Stage 1 | Full | Half | Quarter |
| ------- | ---- | ---- | ------- |
| Butter  | 376g | 188g | 93.9g   |
| Sweetex | 376g | 188g | 93.9g   |

Stages 2 and 3 are unchanged.

- Butter is ~16% water, so hold back roughly 10% of the stage 3 milk and add the rest to feel.
- Keep everything at cool room temperature. The extra butter makes the 15-minute whip more likely to go greasy rather than fluffy in a warm room.
- Expect slightly less heat tolerance — butter softens around 30 °C where Sweetex stays plastic well above it. Fine for same-day service; less forgiving in a lit display case or on a warm day.
- If it still reads as not quite real, push to 60/40 or 70/30 before doing anything else. 50/50 is only a ~6.5 point shift and is a deliberately conservative first step.

## How to Make

**Batter.** Combine the sugar, cake flour, Sweetex, cocoa, baking powder, baking soda and eggs in the mixer bowl. Mix 3 minutes on low speed.

Add the first portion of hot water **very slowly**, along with the salt. Once incorporated, mix 3 minutes on medium.

Add the second portion of hot water **very slowly**, along with the whole milk. Once incorporated, mix 3 minutes on medium.

Scale into greased and lined pans and bake at **180 °C** (original: 360 °F ≈ 182 °C) until a toothpick comes out clean.

**Frosting.** Mix the butter, Sweetex and confectioners' sugar together slowly, then whip 3 minutes. Slowly work in the second addition of confectioners' sugar, Sweetex and butter, then mix on high. Add the milk **very slowly**. Whip on high for 15 minutes.

## Notes

- **This is a high-ratio cake.** Sugar exceeds flour (133%) and total liquid is very high, which is why it depends on **Sweetex** — an emulsified high-ratio shortening. Ordinary shortening or butter will not carry this much sugar and liquid; the batter will break. Do not substitute.
- **Two separate hot-water additions — confirmed, not a transcription error.** The hot water goes in twice, in two distinct "very slowly" stages, and the total is **2× the tabled amount** (3785 mL for the 8 qt batch). The identical repeated line on the original card is correct as written. This is standard high-ratio method: liquid staged in rather than added at once.

  | | Column | Card as written | Used here |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Batter Sweetex | ¾ qt | 7¼ oz → 206g | 202g |
  | Batter Sweetex | ½ qt | 5 oz → 142g | 135g |
  | Frosting sugar, stage 1 | Quarter | 9 oz → 255g | 248g |
  | Frosting milk | Half | ⅓ cup → 79mL | 89mL |
  | Frosting milk | Quarter | ⅕ cup → 47mL | 44mL |
- **Liquids are volume, not weight.** Eggs, water and milk were specified in quarts on the original, so they are given in millilitres — an exact conversion. Converting them to grams would require assuming a density and would introduce error into a recipe that has none. If you want to weigh them: water 1 mL ≈ 1 g, whole milk ≈ 1.03 g/mL, whole egg ≈ 1.03 g/mL.
- **Rumford** is a specific baking powder — aluminium-free, monocalcium phosphate, fast-acting. Paired here with an equal weight of baking soda against a heavy dose of natural cocoa. Substituting a slow-acting double-acting powder will change the crumb.
- **Cocoa is Dutch-process (alkalised)** — Valrhona, as used in the bakery. It is not acidic, so the large soda addition is not there to neutralise the cocoa; it is reacting with the Rumford's acid phosphate, and doing the dark-colour and tenderising work that heavy soda does in a devil's-food style cake. **Do not substitute a natural (non-alkalised) cocoa** — it would throw the acid balance off against this much soda.

## Buy

- **Sweetex** — high-ratio emulsified shortening; standard bakery supply item, sold in 50 lb cubes. No true home-scale equivalent.
- **Rumford Baking Powder** — widely available; keep it separate from the general-purpose tin.
