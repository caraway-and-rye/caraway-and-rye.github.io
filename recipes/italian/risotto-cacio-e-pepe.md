# Risotto cacio e pepe

Massimo Bottura's risotto, created in 2012 to use up the Parmigiano Reggiano damaged by the Emilia-Romagna earthquakes. No onion, no garlic, no butter, no wine — the cheese does all the work, including the seasoning.

Serves 4. Start the day before.

### Ingredients:

### For the Parmigiano stock:

- 1kg Parmigiano Reggiano, 30 months aged, grated
- 2l mineral water

### For the pepper distillate (optional, see notes):

- 5g white pepper
- 2.5g each Szechuan, Jamaican, Sarawak and wild pepper
- 500ml mineral water

### For the risotto:

- 300g Vialone Nano rice
- extra virgin olive oil
- 50g black pepper oil (Villa Manodori Essenziale)
- the Parmigiano stock and cream, from above

### To serve:

- nothing — it goes out flat on a warm plate, on its own

### How to make the stock (the day before):

Put the grated **Parmigiano** and the **water** in a large pan and bring it up slowly to 80°C. Do not let it go past 90°C or the cheese will split. When it turns stringy, take it off the heat and let it cool to room temperature. Bring it back to 80°C and cool it again.

Cover and chill overnight, at least 8 hours. It will settle into three layers: a **Parmigiano cream** on top, milky **stock** in the middle, protein solids at the bottom. Lift off the cream and keep it, strain the stock, throw away the solids.

### How to make the pepper distillate:

Crush the **peppercorns**, vacuum seal them with the **water** and leave at 20°C for 24 hours. Distil in a rotary evaporator at 24°C. Keep the distillate cold in a spray bottle.

### How to make the risotto:

Keep the **Parmigiano stock** warm in a pan. Toast the **rice** in a separate wide pan in a little **olive oil**, then start adding the stock a ladle at a time, stirring, as for any risotto.

About three quarters of the way through, start working in the **Parmigiano cream** and the **black pepper oil**, a spoon at a time, adding the rest as the rice comes to the finish. Let it sit a minute in the pan, still stirring.

Pour onto the plate and spread it flat. Spray the **pepper distillate** over the top at the table.

### Notes:

Don't salt it. The stock is salty enough on its own.

The rotary evaporator is the one step that stays in the restaurant. Without it, finish with the pepper oil and a grind of the mixed peppercorns over the plate — good, but not the same dish.
